🗺️ Where We Are

Lecture 7: every purely nondeterministic stationary series is an MA(\(\infty\)) in its least-squares innovations, \[x_t = \mu + \sum_{j\ge0}\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\]

From \(T\) observations we cannot estimate infinitely many \(\psi_j\). The ARMA model replaces the sequence \(\psi_j\) by a ratio of two short polynomials, \(\psi(L) = \theta(L)/\phi(L)\). Three questions follow, and they are the whole lecture: which orders \((p, q)\)? how to estimate the coefficients? how to forecast with them?

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 3.

❓ The Question

Where will the yield curve be in a year?

The term spread, 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield, is one of the best recession predictors in macroeconomics. In September 2026 it stood near 0.9 percentage points. What is the best forecast for September 2027, and how wide must an honest 95% interval be?

A forecast without an interval is half an answer. ARMA models give both, and they give them from the series’ own past alone.

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Tsay (2010), ch. 2.

📐 The ARMA(\(p,q\)) Model

Definition

\(x_t\) is ARMA(\(p,q\)) if \(\ \phi(L)(x_t - \mu) = \theta(L)\varepsilon_t\), where \[\phi(L) = 1 - \phi_1L - \dots - \phi_pL^p, \qquad \theta(L) = 1 + \theta_1L + \dots + \theta_qL^q,\] with \(\varepsilon_t\) white noise \((0, \sigma^2)\) and no common roots of \(\phi\) and \(\theta\).

  • Stationary (causal) iff all roots of \(\phi(z) = 0\) lie outside the unit circle. Then \(\psi(L) = \theta(L)/\phi(L)\) and \(\sum|\psi_j| < \infty\)
  • Invertible iff all roots of \(\theta(z) = 0\) lie outside the unit circle. Then \(\varepsilon_t\) is the LS innovation (lecture 7)
  • AR(1): root \(1/\rho\), so \(|\rho| < 1\). MA(1): root \(-1/\theta\), so \(|\theta| < 1\)

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 3.

📐 Second Moments

model \(\gamma_0\) autocorrelation \(\rho_j\)
AR(1) \(\sigma^2/(1 - \rho^2)\) \(\rho^j\)
MA(1) \((1 + \theta^2)\sigma^2\) \(\rho_1 = \theta/(1 + \theta^2)\), \(\ \rho_j = 0\) for \(j \ge 2\)
AR(\(p\)) from Yule–Walker \(\rho_j = \phi_1\rho_{j-1} + \dots + \phi_p\rho_{j-p}\), \(j \ge 1\)
ARMA(1,1) \(\dfrac{1 + 2\phi\theta + \theta^2}{1 - \phi^2}\sigma^2\) \(\rho_1 = \dfrac{(1 + \phi\theta)(\phi + \theta)}{1 + 2\phi\theta + \theta^2}\), \(\ \rho_j = \phi\rho_{j-1}\), \(j \ge 2\)

The AR part fixes how the ACF decays; the MA part fixes only the first \(q\) values.

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 3.

📐 The Partial Autocorrelation Function

Definition

The PACF at lag \(k\), \(\alpha_{kk}\), is the last coefficient of the linear projection \[\operatorname{Proj}(x_t \mid 1, x_{t-1}, \dots, x_{t-k}) = \alpha_{k0} + \alpha_{k1}x_{t-1} + \dots + \alpha_{kk}x_{t-k}.\]

It is the correlation of \(x_t\) and \(x_{t-k}\) net of the lags in between. For an AR(\(p\)) and \(k > p\), the projection is the model itself, so \(\alpha_{kk} = 0\).

ACF PACF
AR(\(p\)) decays cuts off after \(p\)
MA(\(q\)) cuts off after \(q\) decays
ARMA(\(p,q\)) decays after lag \(q\) decays after lag \(p\)

✏️ Yule–Walker for an AR(2)

Multiply \(x_t - \mu = \phi_1(x_{t-1} - \mu) + \phi_2(x_{t-2} - \mu) + \varepsilon_t\) by \(x_{t-j} - \mu\), \(j = 1, 2\), take expectations and divide by \(\gamma_0\): \[\rho_1 = \phi_1 + \phi_2\rho_1, \qquad \rho_2 = \phi_1\rho_1 + \phi_2.\] Suppose the sample gives \(\hat\rho_1 = 0.8\) and \(\hat\rho_2 = 0.5\).

Solve the two linear equations: \[\hat\phi_1 = \frac{\rho_1(1 - \rho_2)}{1 - \rho_1^2} = \frac{0.8 \cdot 0.5}{0.36} = 1.111, \qquad \hat\phi_2 = \frac{\rho_2 - \rho_1^2}{1 - \rho_1^2} = \frac{0.5 - 0.64}{0.36} = -0.389.\]

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 3.

✏️ The AR(2) Fit: Roots and Weights

Stationary? The AR(2) triangle: \(\phi_1 + \phi_2 = 0.722 < 1\), \(\ \phi_2 - \phi_1 = -1.5 < 1\), \(\ |\phi_2| < 1\). Yes.

Shape. \(\phi_1^2 + 4\phi_2 = -0.321 < 0\), so the roots of \(\phi(z)\) are complex with \(|z| = 1/\sqrt{-\phi_2} = 1.604 > 1\): a damped cycle.

Wold weights from \(\psi_j = \phi_1\psi_{j-1} + \phi_2\psi_{j-2}\): \(\ \psi_1 = 1.111\), \(\psi_2 = 0.846\), \(\psi_3 = 0.508\).

Innovation variance: \(\sigma^2 = \gamma_0(1 - \phi_1\rho_1 - \phi_2\rho_2) = 0.306\,\gamma_0\).

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 3.

📐 Estimating an AR

  • OLS of \(x_t\) on \(1, x_{t-1}, \dots, x_{t-p}\), conditioning on the first \(p\) observations. It is the sample analogue of the projection of lecture 7, so it is consistent under ergodicity, and asymptotically normal: for AR(1), \[\sqrt T(\hat\rho - \rho) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\ 1 - \rho^2).\]
  • Yule–Walker solves \(\rho_j = \sum_i\phi_i\rho_{j-i}\) with sample autocorrelations. Same limit as OLS, and the fit is always stationary
  • With Gaussian errors, OLS is the conditional MLE

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 5, and Tsay (2010), ch. 2.

📐 Estimating MA and ARMA

The regressors \(\varepsilon_{t-j}\) are not observed, so OLS is out. Conditional sum of squares: set \(\varepsilon_0 = \dots = 0\), build residuals recursively, \[\varepsilon_t(\beta) = (x_t - \mu) - \sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i(x_{t-i} - \mu) - \sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}(\beta),\] and minimise \(\sum_t\varepsilon_t(\beta)^2\) numerically. The start-up error dies out only if \(\theta(L)\) is invertible: lecture 7’s picture again.

Gaussian MLE adds the density of the first observations (Kalman filter, Hamilton ch. 5). Under i.i.d. non-Gaussian errors it is a QMLE, still consistent and asymptotically normal.

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 5.

💻 Conditional Least Squares by Hand

Code 
x <- 1 + arima.sim(list(ar = 0.8, ma = 0.4), n = 500)   # ARMA(1,1), mu = 1
css <- function(b, x) {                                  # b = (mu, phi, theta)
  e <- numeric(length(x))
  for (t in 2:length(x)) e[t] <- (x[t] - b[1]) - b[2] * (x[t - 1] - b[1]) - b[3] * e[t - 1]
  sum(e[-1]^2)
}
fit <- optim(c(mean(x), 0, 0), css, x = x, method = "BFGS")
ref <- arima(x, order = c(1, 0, 1), method = "CSS")
rbind(truth = c(1, 0.8, 0.4), by_hand = fit$par,
      stats_arima = coef(ref)[c("intercept", "ar1", "ma1")]) |>
  `colnames<-`(c("mu", "phi", "theta")) |> round(3)
               mu   phi theta
truth       1.000 0.800 0.400
by_hand     0.715 0.785 0.441
stats_arima 0.715 0.785 0.441

A few lines of R reproduce the library’s estimator. The library’s method = "ML" adds the exact first-observation term.

🔁 The Box–Jenkins Cycle

  1. Identify. Transform to stationarity; read tentative \((p, q)\) from the ACF and PACF; shortlist with AIC/BIC (lecture 4)
  2. Estimate by OLS, CSS or (Q)MLE
  3. Check that residuals look like white noise. The Ljung–Box statistic \[Q(m) = T(T + 2)\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{\hat\rho_k^2(\hat\varepsilon)}{T - k} \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2(m - p - q)\] under correct specification. If it rejects, return to step 1
  4. Forecast, and evaluate out of sample (lecture 5)

Parsimony is the rule: an ARMA(1,1) often does what an AR(5) does, with three parameters fewer.

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Box and Jenkins (1970) and Tsay (2010), ch. 2.

📐 Optimal Forecasts

Under squared loss the best forecast is the conditional mean; for a linear model with LS innovations it is the projection. From the Wold form, the future splits into known and unknown innovations: \[x_{T+h} = \mu + \underbrace{\sum_{j\ge h}\psi_j\varepsilon_{T+h-j}}_{\text{known at } T} + \underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\psi_j\varepsilon_{T+h-j}}_{\text{future}}\]

\[\hat x_{T+h|T} = \mu + \sum_{j\ge h}\psi_j\varepsilon_{T+h-j}, \qquad MSE(h) = \sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\psi_j^2 \ \xrightarrow[h\to\infty]{}\ \gamma_0.\] Forecasts revert to \(\mu\); the MSE rises to the unconditional variance. A 95% interval is \(\hat x_{T+h|T} \pm 1.96\sqrt{MSE(h)}\) under Gaussian errors, ignoring estimation error.

Not in the 2016 notes: added from Hamilton (1994), ch. 4.

✏️ AR(1) Forecast of the Spread

Monthly spread: \(\mu = 1.5\), \(\rho = 0.95\), \(\sigma = 0.3\), and an inverted curve today, \(x_T = -0.5\).

\[\hat x_{T+h|T} = \mu + \rho^h(x_T - \mu), \qquad MSE(h) = \sigma^2\,\frac{1 - \rho^{2h}}{1 - \rho^2}.\]

\(h\) \(\hat x_{T+h\mid T}\) \(\sqrt{MSE(h)}\) 95% interval
1 \(-0.40\) \(0.30\) \([-0.99,0.19]\)
6 \(0.03\) \(0.65\) \([-1.25,1.31]\)
12 \(0.42\) \(0.81\) \([-1.16,2.00]\)
\(\infty\) \(1.50\) \(0.96\) \([-0.38,3.38]\)

After a year less than half the gap to \(\mu\) is closed; the interval is at 84% of its final width.

🧠 Think-Pair-Share

\(x_t = 2 + \varepsilon_t + 0.6\,\varepsilon_{t-1}\), \(\ \sigma^2 = 1\), and the last innovation was \(\hat\varepsilon_T = 0.5\).

  1. Compute \(\hat x_{T+1|T}\), \(\hat x_{T+2|T}\) and their MSEs.
  2. Why is the two-step forecast no better than the unconditional mean?
  3. A series has PACF \(0.90, -0.40, 0.02, 0.01\) and a slowly decaying, oscillating ACF. Which model would you try first?

✅ Think-Pair-Share: Solution

  1. \(\hat x_{T+1|T} = 2 + 0.6 \cdot 0.5 = 2.3\), \(\ MSE(1) = \sigma^2 = 1\). \(\ \hat x_{T+2|T} = 2\), \(\ MSE(2) = (1 + 0.6^2)\sigma^2 = 1.36\).
  1. \(\psi_j = 0\) for \(j \ge 2\): nothing known at \(T\) affects \(x_{T+2}\). The MSE has already reached \(\gamma_0 = 1.36\). An MA(\(q\)) has memory of exactly \(q\) periods.
  1. PACF cuts off after lag 2 and the ACF decays: an AR(2). The negative \(\alpha_{22}\) with an oscillating ACF points to complex roots, as in the worked example.

💰 Case Study: The Forecast Fan

What the data say: the PACF suggests AR(2), but AR(2) fails Ljung–Box; ARMA(1,1) has the lowest BIC and white residuals. Its forecast creeps towards the 1.53 pp mean, with a 12-month interval almost four points wide. The weak point is one constant mean for 1982–2026: the 2022–24 inversion was the longest in the sample.

📋 Key Formulas

ARMA(\(p,q\)) \(\phi(L)(x_t - \mu) = \theta(L)\varepsilon_t\), \(\ \psi(L) = \theta(L)/\phi(L)\)
Stationary / invertible roots of \(\phi(z)\) / \(\theta(z)\) outside the unit circle
Yule–Walker, AR(\(p\)) \(\rho_j = \sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i\rho_{j-i}\), \(\ \sigma^2 = \gamma_0(1 - \sum_i\phi_i\rho_i)\)
PACF \(\alpha_{kk}\) = last coefficient of the projection on \(k\) lags
Ljung–Box \(Q(m) = T(T + 2)\sum_{k\le m}\hat\rho_k^2/(T - k) \sim \chi^2(m - p - q)\)
Forecast \(\hat x_{T+h\mid T} = \mu + \sum_{j\ge h}\psi_j\varepsilon_{T+h-j}\)
Forecast MSE \(\sigma^2\sum_{j<h}\psi_j^2 \to \gamma_0\)
AR(1) \(\mu + \rho^h(x_T - \mu)\), \(\ \sigma^2(1 - \rho^{2h})/(1 - \rho^2)\)

📋 Summary

  • ARMA describes the Wold weights with two short polynomials; roots outside the unit circle give stationarity and invertibility
  • ACF cut-off → MA order; PACF cut-off → AR order; mixed decay → ARMA, shortlisted by BIC
  • AR by OLS or Yule–Walker; MA and ARMA by conditional least squares or (Q)MLE
  • Ljung–Box on residuals is the specification check
  • Forecasts revert to the mean at the speed of the \(\psi_j\); the MSE rises to \(\gamma_0\)
  • The term spread: ARMA(1,1), slow reversion, a 12-month interval about four points wide
